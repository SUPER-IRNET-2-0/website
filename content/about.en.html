Opening a new era of wide-field near-infrared astronomy across five continents.

SUPER-IRNET-2.0 is supported by the JSPS Core-to-Core Program (A. Advanced Research Networks) and builds on SUPER-IRNET, which ran from 2021 to 2025. It strategically connects research in wide-field near-infrared surveys around the world and establishes a next-generation international hub from which Japan and early-career researchers can lead the field.

Why wide-field near-infrared surveys?

How did the first objects in the universe form, and how did galaxies and black holes grow? With its exceptional field of view, Subaru Telescope has found numerous objects in the universe 13 billion years ago at visible wavelengths. JWST now reveals the universe only a few hundred million years after the Big Bang in remarkable detail, but its field of view is limited.

To uncover the full picture of the early universe, we must observe redshifted light in the near-infrared over wider areas, to greater depth, and at higher spatial resolution. SUPER-IRNET-2.0 combines wide surveys from space with adaptive optics and versatile instruments on the ground to overcome the barriers of wavelength coverage and Earth's atmosphere.

Three pillars

01

Coordinated observations from space and the ground

We connect ESA's Euclid and NASA's Roman with Subaru and other ground-based telescopes, combining their complementary survey area, depth, resolution, and wavelength coverage.

02

Realizing ULTIMATE-Subaru

Through multilateral technical collaboration, we develop and operate ULTIMATE, a next-generation adaptive optics system that will greatly expand Subaru's wide-field near-infrared capability.

03

Pioneering science with university-led facilities

We advance wide-field near-infrared surveys and time-domain astronomy with SWIMS on the TAO 6.5 m telescope in Chile and PRIME in South Africa.

A five-continent, nine-country network

The original network linking Japan, the USA, France, Australia, and Taiwan now expands to include the UK, Korea, Chile, and South Africa. By connecting experts in space missions, ground-based telescopes, instrumentation, and data analysis, the program maximizes scientific discovery through multilateral and multi-wavelength collaboration.

A lasting hub connecting ground and space

SUPER-IRNET established a ground-based research hub at the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan, centered on Subaru Telescope. SUPER-IRNET-2.0 adds a new space-mission research hub centered at the JAXA Institute of Space and Astronautical Science and strengthens the connection between the two.

Discoveries from Euclid, Roman, ULTIMATE, SWIMS, and PRIME will feed into missions of the 2030s, including JASMINE, GREX-PLUS, and PRIMA. By bringing ground- and space-based communities together, the program will nurture early-career researchers capable of leading major international projects and establish a world-class network that continues beyond the funding period.
